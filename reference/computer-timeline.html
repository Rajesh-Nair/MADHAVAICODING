Machines That Count

From beads to billions of switches. Each step made counting faster and the machine smaller.

  1. 🧮Long ago

    Abacus

    Beads on rods, using place value (ones, tens, hundreds).

  2. ⚙️1642

    Pascaline

    Pascal's gear adder. Gears do the carrying.

  3. 🏭1820s–1840s

    Difference Engine

    Babbage's automatic calculator design. Built from his plans in 1991, and it worked.

  4. 💡1945

    ENIAC

    Electronic: about 18,000 vacuum tubes, 30 tons, more than 1,000× faster than earlier machines.

  5. 🔌1947

    Transistor

    A tiny, reliable switch (Bell Labs, Nobel Prize 1956).

  6. 🔲1971

    Intel 4004

    First microprocessor: a computer brain on one chip, about 2,300 transistors.

  7. 🚀Today

    Modern chips

    Tens of billions of transistors (Apple M4: about 28 billion).

The big idea: smaller switches → more switches on a chip → faster, smaller, cheaper computers.

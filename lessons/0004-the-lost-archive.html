Mission: The Lost Archive 🛰️

A solar storm has wiped the memory of Mission Control. Only you can rebuild it, in your own handwriting.

📅 About 1 week (one fragment a day)⏱ 15–25 min per fragment📓 Coder's Journal = your Field Guide✏️ Pencil + coloured pencils

Prints all the tasks and questions, without the code boxes, so you can work on paper too.

What this is: a self-directed review of Chapter 1. Madhav re-reads lessons 1.1–1.3, explores the "Explore more" links, and writes his own notes in his journal. Writing notes from memory in his own words builds long-term memory far better than re-reading.
How to run it:
(1) Kick-off, together (10 min): read the story, the Safe Explorer rules and the "How to make a Field Guide page" recipe. Set up the Contents page with him.
(2) On his own: about one fragment a day. This counts as his homework for the week. Each fragment code he unlocks shows a vault letter. Progress is saved in the browser, so use the same device and browser each day.
(3) Final session, the Archive Hearing (about 30–45 min): he closes the screen and answers your questions using only his notebook. If his notes can answer them, they're good notes. Questions and a rubric are in teacher/0004-the-lost-archive-key.md.
Watch for: copying sentences word for word. Gently push "close the page, then write". Short and in his own words beats long and copied.

🛡️ Safe Explorer rules

  1. Use the course pages and the links on them. They're checked and safe.
  2. Want to search for something else? Ask your teacher first, and search together.
  3. No AI chatbots on your own. Do this mission with your own brain! (You can ask AI questions with your teacher.)
  4. If you see anything strange or upsetting, close the tab and tell your teacher. You won't be in trouble.
  5. Some pages are written for grown-ups. That's OK: read the parts you can, and ask about the rest.

📝 How to make a Field Guide page

Every fragment gets one page in your journal. Follow this recipe:

  1. Title + date at the top, e.g. "Fragment 1: The Counting Machines".
  2. The big idea, in your own words. Read, then close the page, then write 2–3 sentences. No copying!
  3. Key facts: the answers to the main questions. Dates, names and numbers.
  4. Draw it! A machine, a diagram or a mini timeline. Labels make drawings smarter.
  5. ⭐ Wow fact: one amazing thing you found in your own exploring.
  6. Where I found it: the website names, e.g. "Lesson 1.1, Britannica Kids".
  7. ❓ I still wonder…: one question you still have.

Example page (about something that is not in this mission):

The Moons of Mars · 3 Nov
Big idea: Mars has two small moons. Scientists think they might be captured asteroids.
Key facts: 2 moons · Phobos (bigger) · Deimos (smaller)
Drawing: 🔴 Mars with two lumpy potatoes going round it
⭐ Wow: Phobos goes round Mars three times every day!
Found it: NASA Mars moons page
❓ Wonder: Could astronauts land on Phobos?

🗂️ Before you start: leave the first 2 pages of your Field Guide section empty. That's your Contents page. Number every page in the corner. At the end, you'll list every fragment and its page number, so future-you can find anything fast.

💾 The 7 Data Fragments

Fragment 1 · The Counting Machines 🧮⚙️

Where to look: Lesson 1.1 · Britannica Kids: abacus · Science Museum: Babbage

Main questions

  1. What clever idea makes an abacus work? (Hint: ones, tens, hundreds…)
  2. What did Pascal's machine use to do its "carrying"?
  3. What happened to Babbage's Difference Engine in 1991?

🔍 Deep Scan (explore the links!)

  • Which ancient people probably invented the abacus, and where did they live?
  • Name 2 places where people still use an abacus today.
  • Work on Babbage's first engine stopped in 1833. Why? (Science Museum)

🔑 Fragment code: In what year did Pascal build his adding machine?

Fragment 2 · The Electronic Giant 💡

Where to look: Lesson 1.1 · CHM: ENIAC · CHM: ENIAC programmers

Main questions

  1. What kind of switches did ENIAC use, and about how many?
  2. How big was ENIAC? (Weight, and how much space it filled)
  3. What went wrong every day or two, and why was it a big job to fix?

🔍 Deep Scan

  • ENIAC's first programmers were six women. Write down at least 2 of their names.
  • ENIAC had no keyboard. How did they program it?
  • By the time ENIAC retired in 1955, it had done more calculations than… whom? (CHM: ENIAC)

🔑 Fragment code: How many tons did ENIAC weigh?

Fragment 3 · The Tiny Switch 🔌🔲

Where to look: Lesson 1.1 · CHM: Inventing the Transistor · Bell Labs: Nobel Prize · Timeline cheat sheet

Main questions

  1. When and where was the transistor invented?
  2. Why was a transistor better than a vacuum tube? Give 2 reasons.
  3. What was special about the Intel 4004 in 1971?

🔍 Deep Scan

  • Which three scientists won the 1956 Nobel Prize for the transistor?
  • Look at the photo of the first transistor on the CHM page. Draw it in your Field Guide!
  • Calculator time: a modern chip has about 28 billion transistors. How many times more is that than the Intel 4004?

🔑 Fragment code: How many transistors did the Intel 4004 have?

Fragment 4 · Brains and Muscles 🧠💪

Where to look: Lesson 1.2 · Video: CPU, Memory, Input & Output · Britannica Kids: computer · Cheat sheet

Main questions

  1. What's the difference between hardware and software? Give an example of each.
  2. Draw Input → Process → Output for one machine in your house.
  3. Explain CPU vs GPU using the chef idea (or your own idea!). How many cores does your computer's CPU have? (Check your Mission Log 2.)

🔍 Deep Scan

  • Watch the video. What job does a computer's memory do?
  • Why are GPUs so good for games and videos? Use the word pixels.

🔑 Fragment code: How many paintballs did the "GPU" robot fire all at once?

Fragment 5 · The Dreamers and the Rule-Followers 💭♟️

Where to look: Lesson 1.3 (Eras 1 and 2) · Britannica Kids: Alan Turing · CHM: Chatbots Decoded · IBM: Deep Blue

Main questions

  1. What question did Alan Turing ask in 1950? How does his "imitation game" work?
  2. Who came up with the words "artificial intelligence", and where?
  3. How did ELIZA and Deep Blue work? Did they learn, or follow rules?

🔍 Deep Scan

  • Find one other famous thing Alan Turing did. (Britannica Kids)
  • ELIZA pretended to do a job. Which job? Why was its creator worried about how people treated it? (CHM)
  • What was an "AI winter", and why did it happen?

🔑 Fragment code: In what year was the Dartmouth workshop held?

Fragment 6 · Machines That Learn 🧬🖼️

Where to look: Lesson 1.3 (Era 3) · DeepMind: AlphaGo · Video: What is Machine Learning?

Main questions

  1. Why is "learning from examples" better than writing rules? Use your cat game from Lesson 1.3!
  2. The Perceptron was invented in 1958. Why did it have to wait so long to become useful?
  3. What did AlexNet learn in 2012, and what helped it learn so fast?

🔍 Deep Scan

  • One AlphaGo move was so surprising that experts thought it was a mistake. It had a 1 in ______ chance of being played. (DeepMind)
  • About how many people around the world watched AlphaGo play Lee Sedol?
  • Watch the Machine Learning video. Explain it in one sentence a 7-year-old would understand.

🔑 Fragment code: What was the number of AlphaGo's famous surprise move?

Fragment 7 · Machines That Talk 👀🗨️

Where to look: Lesson 1.3 (Era 4) · Video: How Chatbots and LLMs Work · Britannica Kids: AI · AI cheat sheet

Main questions

  1. What do the letters G, P and T in GPT stand for? And LLM?
  2. When did ChatGPT arrive? Name 2 other AI assistants.
  3. What is a "hallucination", and what should you do about it?

🔍 Deep Scan

  • Watch the Chatbots video. How does a chatbot decide what words to say next? Use your own words.
  • ELIZA (1966) vs ChatGPT (2022): how are they different? Use the words "rules" and "learned".
  • Find 3 places AI is used, from Britannica Kids or your own life.

🔑 Fragment code: In what year was the Transformer ("Attention Is All You Need") published?

⭐ Bonus Fragment · Uncharted Space

Real explorers find things nobody told them about! With your teacher, find one machine, inventor or AI moment that is NOT in any lesson. Some ideas to search for: Ada Lovelace, the first video game, the Apollo Guidance Computer, robots on Mars.

Give it its own Field Guide page, and write where you found it.

  2. Contents page. Fill in your front page: each fragment's title and page number.
  3. Archivist badge. Design your own mission badge on the last page. 🎖️
